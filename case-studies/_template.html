Overview

Problem

What was broken, for whom, in one or two lines.

Solution

What you designed, in one or two lines.

Outcome

The result. A number if you have one, an honest proxy if you don't.

The problem

Context in one short paragraph. Who uses this, what they were trying to do, and what got in the way. Then why it mattered to the business: lost revenue, support load, churn, a missed goal.

Starting problem statement [User] needs a way to [goal] because [insight], but currently [obstacle].

Constraints worth naming: timeline, platform, tech stack, accessibility requirements.

My role

Say exactly what you did. Use "I" for your work and "we" for the team's.

  • Planned and ran [n] user interviews
  • Led synthesis and wrote the reframed problem statement
  • Designed flows, wireframes and the high-fidelity prototype
  • Ran [n] rounds of usability testing
  • Built the front end in [stack] / partnered with engineering on handoff

Research

Which methods you used and why each fit the question. Keep this short; the insights are the point.

  • 6 user interviews
  • Survey · 40 responses
  • Competitive analysis
  • Heuristic audit
  • Analytics review

What I learned

01

An insight explains why, not just what.

"Users skip onboarding" is an observation. "Users skip onboarding because they want proof of value first" is an insight.

"A real quote from a participant that backs this up."

02

Second key insight.

Two or three insights total. Leave the rest of the findings in your notes.

"Supporting quote."

03

Third key insight.

Ideally one of these surprised you and changed the direction.

"Supporting quote."

Affinity map, journey map or persona · one strong artifact, not five

Reframe

How research changed the problem. This shift is often the most interesting part of the whole story.

I thought the problem was

"Users need more features on the dashboard."

→
It turned out to be

"Users can't find the one thing they came to do."

How might we [let users do X within Y]?

Exploration

Show two or three genuinely different directions and why you picked one. Showing what you rejected shows judgment.

Iteration

Each pair is: what testing showed, what you changed, and the evidence. Copy the block for every meaningful change, ordered by impact.

Testing showed
4 of 5 participants missed the primary action.
So I changed
Promoted it to a full-width button and removed two competing links.
Result
All 5 found it in round two, in under 10 seconds.
